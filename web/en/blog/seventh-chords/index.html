Seventh chords: how they're built and when to use them

The seventh is the fourth note of the chord. It makes the sound richer and full of intention. Here you'll see where it comes from, the difference between 7 and 7M, and build any seventh chord.

Where the seventh comes from

Remember the “skip one, take one” trick from the triads? Keep stacking one more third and you get to the seventh:

1 – 3 – 5 – 7

In C: C (D) E (F) G (A) B. This four-note chord is called four-note chord. The name “seventh” comes from B being the 7th note counting from C.

The seventh can be major (11 half steps, a half step below the octave) or minor (10 half steps, a whole step below the octave). Combining this with major, minor and diminished triads gives the five most common seventh chords.

C7: the dominant seventh chord

Triad major + seventh minor. When the chord symbol just says “7”, this is the seventh: minor. It's the most important chord in tonal music because it creates tension and wants to move to another chord (G7 → C, D7 → G, A7 → D…).

C7M: major seventh

Triad major + seventh major. In Brazil it's written 7M; in other countries it appears as maj7 or Δ. It sounds light, open, very common in bossa nova.

⚠️ Heads up The difference between C7 e C7M is a single key: B♭ or B. Play one and then the other and notice how C7 seems to “want to go” somewhere and C7M seems to “rest”.

Cm7: minor seventh

Triad minor + seventh minor. Widely used in MPB, pop, soul and samba.

Cm7(♭5): half-diminished

Triad diminished + seventh minor. Also written Cø. It's the chord on the VII degree of the major scale (Bm7(♭5) in C) and shows up before the V in minor keys.

C°7: diminished seventh

Triad diminished + seventh diminished (9 half steps). It's three minor thirds in a row, so the chord splits the octave into equal parts and repeats every three frets on the guitar. In popular chord symbols, “C°” almost always already means this four-note chord.

Bonus: Cm(7M)

Triad minor + seventh major. Rare but beautiful: it's the classic suspense-scene sound and shows up in descending bass lines (Cm → Cm(7M) → Cm7 → Cm6).

A practical rule for finding the seventh in any chord

  1. Find the root an octave higher.
  2. Go down a whole step (two keys, two frets): that's a minor seventh (for 7, m7 and m7(♭5)).
  3. Go down just half step: that's the major seventh (for 7M).

Why G7 “pulls” toward C

G7 (G B D F) has a tritone between B and F. That interval is unstable. The B wants to rise a half step to C, and the F wants to drop a half step to E. Result: your ear “asks” for the C chord (C E G). That's why the dominant seventh chord shows up right before the return to the tonic in so many songs.

This is called cadence. See the II–V–I progression and much more in diatonic chords.

Seventh chords on guitar

Many seventh chords on guitar are just the plain chord with one finger added or lifted. Classic first-position examples:

ChordFrom the plain chord to the seventh chord
E → E7Lift your finger off the 4th string (D): it rings open and becomes the seventh (D).
A → A7Lift your finger off the 3rd string (G): it rings open and becomes the seventh (G).
Am → Am7Lift your finger off the 3rd string (G).
Em → Em7Play the open 4th string, or put finger 4 on the 3rd fret of the 2nd string (D).
D → D7Change the shape: C on the 1st fret of the 2nd string (the minor seventh of D).
C → C7MLift your finger off the 2nd string (C): it plays open B, the major seventh.

Quick summary
  • Seventh chord = triad + one more third (4 notes).
  • Just “7” = minor seventh: C7 = C E G B♭.
  • 7M = major seventh: C7M = C E G B.
  • m7 = minor + minor seventh; m7(♭5) = diminished + minor seventh; °7 = three minor thirds.
  • Minor seventh: a whole step below the octave. Major seventh: a half step below.
  • The 7 in the dominant has a tritone that pulls toward the tonic (G7 → C).

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between C7 and C7M?

C7 has a minor seventh (B♭) and sounds tense, asking to resolve. C7M has a major seventh (B) and sounds smooth and stable. Only one note changes.

How is a seventh chord built?

Take the triad (1, 3, 5) and add the seventh note up from the root. If the chord symbol just says 7, it's a minor seventh (10 half steps); if it says 7M, it's a major seventh (11 half steps).

What does m7(b5) mean?

Minor chord with a diminished fifth and a minor seventh, also called half-diminished (ø). Example: Bm7(♭5) = B D F A.

Why is the seventh chord called dominant?

Because it shows up naturally on the V degree (the dominant) of the major scale. In C major, the V degree is G, and the chord built there is G7.

What's the difference between diminished and half-diminished?

Both have 1, ♭3 and ♭5. The half-diminished has a minor seventh (Cm7(♭5) with B♭); the diminished has a diminished seventh (C°7 with B𝄫, which sounds like A).
