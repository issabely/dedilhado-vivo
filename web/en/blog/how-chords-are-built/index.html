How a chord is built (the easy way)

A chord is just a stack of thirds. With one simple rule, you can build any major or minor chord from any note, without memorizing anything.

What a chord is

Chord is the sound of three or more different notes played together. The simplest chord has three notes and is called a triad:

  • Root (1): the note that gives the chord its name.
  • Third (3): the note that decides whether it's major or minor.
  • Fifth (5): the note that adds body and stability.

The trick: “skip one, take one”

In the scale, starting from the root, take a note, skip the next, take the one after. In C: C (D) E (F) G. Done: C–E–G is the chord of C major (C).

This works because chords are made of stacked thirds: C→E is a third, E→G is another third.

Major or minor: the size of the thirds

What changes between a major and a minor chord is the order of the thirds:

TypeFormulaThirdsIn CChord symbol
Major1 – 3 – 5major + minor (4 + 3 half steps)C E GC
Minor1 – ♭3 – 5minor + major (3 + 4)C E♭ GCm
Diminished1 – ♭3 – ♭5minor + minor (3 + 3)C E♭ G♭C° or Cdim
Augmented1 – 3 – ♯5major + major (4 + 4)C E G♯C+ or Caug
💡 Tip To turn any major chord into a minor one, lower the third a half step. That's it. C major (C E G) becomes C minor (C E♭ G).

Building on any note

Use the half-step ruler: from the root, count 4 half steps for the major third (or 3 for the minor) and 7 half steps for the fifth. Examples:

D (D major)DF♯A
Dm (D minor)DFA
E (E major)EG♯B
Em (E minor)EGB
G (G major)GBD
A (A major)AC♯E
Am (A minor)ACE
F (F major)FAC

And on guitar, why does the chord have 6 notes?

The guitar has six strings, but the chord still has only three different notes: they show up repeated in octaves. In E minor (Em) with all six strings, the notes are E, B, E, G, B, E: just E, G and B.

Inversions: same triad, different bass note

The notes of a chord can appear in any order. When the lowest note isn't the root, we have an inversion:

PositionBass noteNotes (from bottom to top)Chord symbol
RootCC E GC
1st inversionE (third)E G CC/E
2nd inversionG (fifth)G C EC/G

The slash in a chord symbol (C/E) means “C chord with E in the bass”. See more in how to read chord symbols.

How each type sounds

  • Major: open, happy, resolved.
  • Minor: darker, melancholic.
  • Diminished: tense, unstable, great as a passing chord.
  • Augmented: mysterious, “floating”, common in suspense soundtracks.

Quick summary
  • Triad = root + third + fifth.
  • Major: 1 – 3 – 5 (4 + 3 half steps).
  • Minor: 1 – ♭3 – 5 (3 + 4 half steps).
  • Diminished: 1 – ♭3 – ♭5; augmented: 1 – 3 – ♯5.
  • On the guitar the notes repeat in octaves, but the chord still has 3 notes.
  • Inversion = another chord note in the bass (C/E, C/G).

Frequently asked questions

How is a major chord built?

The root, the major third (4 half steps up) and the perfect fifth (7 half steps up). Example: C = C, E, G.

What's the difference between a major and a minor chord?

Just the third. In minor it's a half step lower: C = C E G; Cm = C E♭ G.

How many notes does a chord have?

A triad has three different notes. Seventh chords have four, and ninth chords five. On the guitar, notes can repeat across several strings.

What does C/E mean in a chord chart?

C major chord with E in the bass (first inversion).
