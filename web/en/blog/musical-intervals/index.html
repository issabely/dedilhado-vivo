Musical intervals: the distance between two notes

Chords are made of intervals. Knowing that a major third is 4 half steps or that a minor seventh is 10 is what lets you build any chord without memorizing.

What an interval is

Interval is the distance between two notes. Its name has two parts:

  • O number (2nd, 3rd, 5th…): how many letters you count, including the first and the last. From C to E: C, D, E = 3 letters = third.
  • A quality (major, minor, perfect, augmented, diminished): the exact size, in half steps.
💡 Tip Always count both ends. C → G: C D E F G = 5 notes, so it's a fifth.

Perfect fifth: the solid foundation

A perfect fifth (7 half steps) is the most stable interval after the octave. It's in almost every chord and is the basis of the power chord of rock.

Major seventh and minor seventh

A seventh is the note that turns a triad into a seventh chord. It has two very common versions:

An easy way to find it: the minor seventh is a whole step down of the octave; the major seventh, a half step down. This is explained hands-on in seventh chords.

Octave: the same note, higher

A octave has 12 half steps and connects two notes with the same name. It sounds so “alike” because the frequency of the upper note is exactly twice of the one below. See more in octave, ninth and extensions.

Why are some “perfect”?

Unison, fourth, fifth and octave are called perfect because they have only one “natural” size in the major scale and sound very stable. If they get a half step bigger, they become augmented; a half step smaller, diminished. The others (2nd, 3rd, 6th and 7th) are major or minor.

The tritone: the interval of tension

At 6 half steps, the tritone splits the octave exactly in half. It sounds unstable and “asks” to resolve. It's what gives the dominant seventh chord its strength (G7 has B and F, a tritone), which pulls toward C.

Interval inversion

If you invert an interval (the bottom note goes on top), the numbers add up to 9 and the quality flips: major ↔ minor, augmented ↔ diminished, perfect stays perfect. Example: C→E is a major third; E→C (the C above) is a minor sixth. 3 + 6 = 9.

Quick summary
  • Interval number = how many letters, counting both ends.
  • Major third = 4 half steps; minor third = 3.
  • Perfect fifth = 7 half steps.
  • Minor seventh = 10 half steps (a whole step below the octave); major seventh = 11 (a half step below).
  • Octave = 12 half steps, double the frequency.

Frequently asked questions

How many half steps are in a major third?

Four. A minor third has three.

What's the difference between a major seventh and a minor seventh?

The major seventh is 11 half steps (a half step below the octave). The minor seventh is 10 half steps (a whole step below the octave). From C: B is the major seventh and B♭ is the minor seventh.

What is a perfect interval?

Unison, fourth, fifth and octave, the most stable intervals. They aren't called major or minor, but perfect, augmented or diminished.

What is a tritone?

An interval of three whole steps (6 half steps), like F–B. It's unstable and creates the tension of the dominant seventh chord.
